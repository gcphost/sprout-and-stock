# Demo video — from one bot to a shared factory

**Length:** 50 seconds  
**Purpose:** Show the game’s real escalation: manual work → robot crew →
automation → co-op. Do not present it as a growing game.

## The flow

| Time | Picture | On-screen copy | What it proves |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0:00–0:04** | Open immediately on one robot carrying a crate through a tiny shop. The player is nearby doing the same kind of work. | **Started with one bot.** | There is hands-on work and a clear small beginning. |
| **0:04–0:12** | Fast, readable cuts: robot collects an input, a machine makes food, a robot stocks a shelf, a till serves a customer. Briefly show the priorities panel only if it reads in under a second. | **Every job needed doing.** | Robots have roles and are actively useful. |
| **0:12–0:20** | Wider shot of a larger crew. Crates move by hand; workers walk long routes; a shelf runs low or crates gather at a busy area. | **Then the crew got busy.** | More robots solve the early game but introduce a real logistics problem. |
| **0:20–0:28** | Hold on the bottleneck for a beat: a robot crosses the same long route again, a crate queue builds, or a production machine waits on an input. | **Walking became the bottleneck.** | Why automation is needed. This must be visible, not explained in UI text alone. |
| **0:28–0:39** | Satisfying build-and-payoff montage: lay conveyors, attach loaders, place a sorter, then show crates travelling between machines and shelves being fed. Include one recognisable chain, such as biomass → Former → meat product or grain → mill → dough → finished food. | **So I automated the flow.** | The player designs a production line rather than merely hiring indefinitely. |
| **0:39–0:44** | Wide shot of the finished operation: robots handle exceptions while the line carries the repeated work. Show customers buying the resulting products. | **From raw inputs to packed shelves.** | The factory and the shop are one system. |
| **0:44–0:48** | The host opens an invite; a friend joins the same shop. Two player characters/cursors immediately take different tasks. | **Invite a friend.** | Co-op is direct and useful. |
| **0:48–0:50** | Both players work in the same busy factory: one expands a line, one clears or stocks a different area. End on the game logo. | **Build the machine together.** | The final fantasy: shared ownership of a growing operation. |

## Edit rules

- Start on gameplay in the first frame. Do not open on a logo, menu, exterior,
  or grow rack.
- Make the manual-to-automated before/after unmistakable. The same route or
  same product chain should appear on both sides of the transition when
  possible.
- Keep captions to one sentence at a time; the action carries the explanation.
- Show the invite only after the automation payoff. Co-op is the extra pair of
  hands for the machine, not a separate mode.
- End with the game name and a simple call to action. Use platform-specific
  copy later (for example, “Play in your browser” or “Wishlist now”).

## Capture checklist

- A clean starter shop with one robot and visible crate carrying.
- A mid-game shop where crew walks are visibly inefficient.
- A completed conveyor/loader/sorter line moving crates between at least two
  stages of production.
- Finished food on a shelf and customers purchasing it.
- A host and guest joining the same shop and performing different tasks.
